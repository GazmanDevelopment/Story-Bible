1. Getting started and signing in

You can use Story Bible in three ways, and all of them show the same data:

  • In Word as a task pane. Install it from the install page, then open it from the Home tab.
  • In Google Docs as a sidebar. See Using Story Bible in Google Docs.
  • In a browser at storybible.huscroft.com.au. No Word or Google Docs needed. The only things missing are the features that need a document beside them: Find, linking a chapter to a document, and Insert name at cursor.

Click Sign in at the top and sign in with your Microsoft account in the window that opens. Your name and a Sign out button then appear in its place.

No Microsoft account yet? You don't need a Microsoft email address. You can create a free Microsoft account using your existing Gmail or other email address, then sign in to Story Bible with it. Microsoft's guide is How to create a new Microsoft account: choose Create one, then enter the email address you already use.

The first time you sign in, you are asked to accept the terms and the privacy policy before you can use the app. Read them with the links on that screen, then click Accept and continue. You are asked again only if the policy is updated.

If nothing happens when you click Sign in in a browser, your browser has probably blocked the pop-up. Allow pop-ups for the site and try again.

Installing on your phone

You can add Story Bible to your phone's home screen so it opens like an app, full screen and without the browser bars. It is the same browser version described above, so it has the same data and the same missing document features.

  • Android (Chrome): open storybible.huscroft.com.au, open the browser menu and choose Install app (or Add to Home screen).
  • iPhone or iPad (Safari): open the same address, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.

Open it from the new Story Bible icon and sign in as usual. It always needs an internet connection; there is no offline mode.

2. Using Story Bible in Google Docs

Story Bible also works as a sidebar in Google Docs, with the same series, characters and timeline as Word and the browser. It is currently available as a private test: it is added to each document with a small script, and the steps are in the Google Docs setup notes. The script belongs to that one document, not to your Google account, so a new document doesn't have it. The easiest way to start a new manuscript is to make a copy of a document that already has Story Bible (File → Make a copy), which keeps the script.

  1. Open your document and choose Story Bible → Open sidebar from the menu bar. The first time, Google asks you to authorise the script; it only reads the text you have selected and inserts names where you ask.
  2. Click Sign in and sign in with your Microsoft account, as in Getting started. If you don't have one, the tip there explains how to create one from your Gmail address.
  3. Use the pane as you would in Word.

  • Find: select a name in your document and click Find to jump to that character or place.
  • Insert name at cursor: open a character and click it. The name replaces whatever you have selected, or goes at the cursor if nothing is selected.
  • Linking a document: click Link to this series, then pick a chapter if you like. The link is remembered with the document, so it is still there next time you open it.
  • Open in New Tab: Story Bible → Open in New Tab opens Story Bible in a browser tab of its own, handy when you want more room. It works like the browser version, so Find and Insert name at cursor are not available there. If no tab opens, your browser blocked the pop-up: click the link in the small window that appears.

3. Setting up a series

Everything lives inside a series. The first time you open the app, click + New series and give it a name. Use the drop-down at the top to switch between series.

Open the Series tab to edit the series:

  • Series name and Premise / notes.
  • Default character fields: the extra fields every character gets, one per line. They start as Build, Skin, Tattoos, Piercings, Distinguishing marks, and Voice & mannerisms. Edit the list to suit your story.
  • Chapters: type a number and title and click Add. In Word or Google Docs, you can link each chapter to the document it lives in, which makes Find and chapter links work.

Click Save after changing the settings. Delete series removes the series and everything in it, so export a backup first if you are unsure.

4. Sharing a series

In the Series tab, under Sharing, you can give someone else access to the whole series - its characters, places, timeline, research and chapters.

  • You must know the exact email address they sign in with. There is no list of other users to search or browse - this is deliberate, for everyone's privacy.
  • Choose Can view (read everything) or Can edit (read and write) for each person, and change it at any time from the drop-down next to their name.
  • Click Remove to revoke someone's access at any time.

If someone shares a series with you, open it from the series drop-down at the top, then check the Series tab: it shows who shared it with you and whether you can view or edit. Click Leave this series at any time to remove your own access - the owner can always share it with you again later.

5. Setting up the timeline

In the Series tab, under Timeline anchor, pick how your story measures time:

  • Relative (no real dates): events are placed by how long after the start they happen, such as 2 years, 3 months. Good for fantasy and invented calendars.
  • Real calendar date: choose a Start date and events show real dates.

Start label names the starting point (for example “Story start”). Events are stored as offsets from the anchor, so changing the anchor later moves the whole timeline with it.

6. Relationship types

When you add a relationship between two characters, the type box suggests these as you type:

married to, partner of, mistress of, lover of, ex of, friend of, best friend of, sibling of, parent of, boss of, colleague of, neighbour of, rival of, flirts with.

They are only suggestions. You can type anything you like. To add your own suggestions for a series (on top of the defaults above), add them under Relationship type suggestions in the Series tab (one per line, up to 200) and save.

7. Adding characters, places and timeline events

Use the Characters, Places and Timeline tabs. Each has an add button at the top; click an existing item to edit it. Only the name (or title) is required.

  • Characters: name, role, aliases, age (a number), appearance, your series' custom fields, likes and limits, backstory and notes.
  • Places: name, where it is, a description, and how it matters to the story.
  • Timeline events: what happens, when (years, months and days from your timeline anchor), and details.

Remember to click Save. If you try to leave with unsaved changes, the app asks first.

8. Linking it all together

  • Relationships: on a character, choose another character, a type, and click Add.
  • Characters in a place or event: on a place or timeline event, click character names to switch them on or off.
  • Research entries can link to characters, places and events the same way.
  • Find (in Word or Google Docs): select a name in your manuscript and click Find to jump to that character or place.

9. Research entries

The Research tab is for notes you gather while writing. Each entry has a title, the date entered, an optional chapter, rich-text notes, and links to characters, places and timeline events.

Images

You can paste, drag in, or insert pictures from the editor's image button.

  • Allowed: PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP. SVG is not accepted.
  • Large images are shrunk automatically to at most 1600 pixels on the longest side.
  • If an image is still bigger than about 2.6 MB after shrinking, it is skipped and a message tells you.
  • An entry can hold about 8 million characters in total, and images count toward that, so a few photos are fine but dozens in one entry may not be. Split them over several entries.

10. Backing up and restoring

In the Series tab, under Backup:

  • Export JSON downloads the current series as a file you can keep anywhere.
  • Import JSON… loads a previously exported file back in.

The server also takes its own nightly backup, but an export of your own is the quickest way to protect a series before a big change.

11. Feedback and logging issues

In the Series tab, under Feedback, click Log Issue for something broken or Log Suggestion for an idea. Give it a title (3–120 characters) and a description (up to 4000). There is a limit of 5 reports per hour. The build version and date of the app are added to every report automatically, so we know which version you were using.

You can see the build version and date yourself at the bottom of the Series tab, under Feedback, and on the report form. Quote them if you contact us another way.

These are public. Issues and suggestions are posted on GitHub where anyone can read them. Never include story content, personal details or passwords.

12. Your data and privacy

Your series belongs to you. You can download all of it at any time (see below) or one series at a time with Export JSON (see Backing up). Read the full privacy policy and terms of use. In the pane, they are linked at the bottom of your account page once you are signed in.

13. Downloading your data and deleting your account

When you are signed in, an Account button appears next to Sign out.

Click it to open your account page. The privacy policy and terms of use are linked at the bottom of this page.

Download my data

Click Download my data to save a file containing your account details and every series you own. Do this first if you want to keep your work.

Delete my account

  1. Read what will happen. Deleting is permanent and takes effect immediately.
  2. Type your email address in the confirmation box.
  3. Click Delete my account and data. You are signed out straight away.

This deletes your account and every series you own, including characters, places, timeline, research and chapters. Anyone you shared a series with loses access to it. You are also removed from series other people shared with you, and your name on their entries is replaced by “Deleted user”.

Inactive accounts

If nobody signs in to your account for 24 months, it is deleted along with every series you own, just as if you had deleted it yourself. Before that we email you at 18, 20 and 23 months, if we have a usable email address for you. To keep your account, open Story Bible (in Word, Google Docs or a browser) and sign in; that starts the 24 months again. To keep a copy of your work instead, use Download my data first.

What is not removed straight away. The server's nightly backups keep a copy until they age out (14 days by default). Issues and suggestions you posted through Feedback are public on GitHub and can't be removed from the pane. You can sign in again later with the same Microsoft account to start fresh.
